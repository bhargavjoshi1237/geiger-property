# Cross-cutting capabilities of property management software (2025–2026): portals, payments, AI, automation, integrations, compliance

Research scope: AppFolio, Buildium, Yardi/RentCafe, Entrata, RealPage, Rent Manager, DoorLoop, Rentvine, Propertyware, ResMan, plus AI specialist EliseAI. Research date: October 2026. Most sources are vendor press releases/product pages (company-reported claims); third-party review blogs are flagged where used. Several primary pages (Yardi blog, Yardi RentCafe product page, Entrata 2026 press release on GlobeNewswire, BusinessWire) returned HTTP 403, so some details come from search-result snippets of those pages and are marked as such.

## Which capabilities are now table stakes vs differentiators?

### Takeaway
The resident portal/app (online rent pay + autopay + maintenance requests with photos + documents + renters insurance + announcements), a basic owner portal (statements, distributions, documents), ACH/card payments, generative "write with AI" text tools, ILS syndication, and an integrations marketplace are table stakes across every vendor in 2025–2026. Differentiators have moved to (a) autonomous AI agents that own whole workflows (leasing, maintenance intake, resident messaging, accounting), (b) workflow-automation engines, (c) AI control centers measuring AI vs human performance, (d) fraud/ID/income verification in leasing, (e) affordable-housing/regulatory compliance depth, and (f) breadth of embedded fintech (split rent, cash networks, credit reporting, deposit alternatives).

### Inferences
- Table stakes (every vendor reviewed): online rent pay with ACH/card, autopay, maintenance requests with photos and status tracking, document access, renters-insurance capture, announcements/SMS reminders, mobile app, owner statements/distributions in a portal, ILS syndication, an integration marketplace/API, and generative writing assistants (Buildium "Write with AI", Rent Manager Orion Writing Assistant).
- Near-table-stakes (common but not universal, often via partners): split/flexible rent (Flex, Livble), cash payment networks (PayNearMe), credit reporting (now effectively required in California for covered landlords), amenity booking, package tracking, resident rewards, deposit alternatives (Obligo).
- Differentiators in 2025–2026: native autonomous agents that close the loop (create work orders, schedule tours, follow up with vendors), AI performance dashboards (Entrata OXP AI Control Center), configurable agent builders (Entrata Agent Studio), AI invoice/bill capture tied to AP, fraud/ID/income verification at application, affordable-housing certification depth (Yardi/RealPage/ResMan territory), and SMB pricing that bundles AI instead of charging add-ons.
- Vendor portals with insurance/COI expiration tracking appear less mature natively in SMB tools and are frequently handled by credentialing add-ons (RealPage Vendor Credentialing, NetVendor, CertLedger).

### Gaps
- No primary source found confirming native PayNearMe for Rent Manager, Rentvine, DoorLoop, Propertyware.
- No vendor documentation found for "certified funds only" toggles, NSF auto-handling, or Section 8/HAP payment posting specifics (these exist in PMS products by common knowledge but were not sourced in this pass).
- No 2025-specific sources on smart access/IoT (e.g., ButterflyMX, SmartRent, Latch integrations) beyond ButterflyMX being an AppFolio Stack launch partner.
- No source on rent-control/rent-stabilization calculators in Yardi/AppFolio; security-deposit-interest support found only in Sage Intacct and LotKeeper.
- No sourced detail on 1099 e-filing in Buildium/AppFolio/Rent Manager (commonly offered, but unverified here); QuickBooks integration specifics not sourced.
- Fair housing–specific features (e.g., AI guardrails, screening criteria consistency) not found in vendor sources this pass.

## What are concrete examples of the AI features and what they do?

### Takeaway
Across 2025–2026 the industry moved from chatbots and copilots to branded "agent workforces": AppFolio Realm-X Performers (Leasing, Maintenance, Resident Messenger) plus Realm-X Flows; RealPage Lumina AI Workforce (Leasing, Resident, Operations, Facilities, Finance — later Spend, Analytics) also powering Buildium; Entrata ELI with OXP AI Control Center and, in 2026, Entrata Pro (ELI Console + Agent Studio); Yardi Virtuoso/Chat IQ; ResMan's Anyone Home–based Leasing Assistant; Rent Manager Orion AI; and EliseAI as the leading independent agent platform (Apollo). Concrete jobs: answer leads 24/7 and book tours, triage maintenance from photos and create work orders, chase delinquencies, code invoices from scanned bills, reconcile accounts, and answer plain-language questions over company data.

### Inferences
- Common AI job taxonomy across vendors (useful for a product roadmap): (1) Leasing agent — 24/7 multichannel lead response, qualification, tour scheduling, CRM prioritization; (2) Maintenance agent — intake via chat/photo, troubleshooting, priority, work-order creation, vendor follow-up; (3) Resident/messaging agent — balances, lease Q&A, payment and renewal nudges, delinquency outreach; (4) Voice/call answering; (5) Finance/AP agent — bill/receipt OCR, GL coding, reconciliation, owner payouts; (6) Operations agent — move-in/out, renewals, audits; (7) Natural-language analytics over company data (ELI Console, Virtuoso, Realm-X); (8) Generative writing/translation; (9) Agent builders and AI-vs-human performance dashboards (Entrata Agent Studio, OXP AI Control Center).
- Rule-based workflow automation (AppFolio Flows: move-in, move-out, reputation, collections) is being merged with agents — the agent is the "doer," flows are the guardrails/orchestration.
- Enterprise vendors (RealPage, Entrata, Yardi) lead on breadth; SMB vendors differentiate on bundling and simplicity (Rentvine included vs DoorLoop add-on vs Buildium Premium-tier).
- Many published metrics (20–35% showing lift, tripled tours, 1-in-4 work orders resolved) are company-reported; no independent benchmarks were found.

### Gaps
- No independent, third-party benchmarks of AI agent outcomes were found; all metrics are vendor-reported.
