# Pasted claim inflates MRI and undersells Geiger

The pasted comparison is wrong as written, though it points in the right direction. Nearly every capability it says Geiger Property lacks is really missing. Geiger has no intercompany accounting, no multi-currency, no VAT/GST and no tiered AP approval routing. It also has no percentage rent, no CPI indexation, no valuation, debt or waterfall modelling, no public API, no enterprise connectors and no AI of any kind. But the claim misdescribes both products. **On the MRI side, it credits Property Management X (PMX) with capabilities MRI sells in other ways.** Some are add-on modules (Enterprise Ledger, Advanced Retail, Investment Accounting) and some are separate suites (the Investment Management suite that came from the Cougar acquisition, and Contract Intelligence). Others are partner products (Nexus, now owned by Bottomline, and AvidXchange), and one is an occupier-side product (AMTdirect). It also overstates several specifics. MRI documents *manual* inter-entity entries, takes retail sales through a tenant portal rather than POS feeds, offers no low-code table or screen builder, has no native Yardi pipeline, and keeps human reviewers in its lease-abstraction flow. **On the Geiger side, it describes a product that no longer exists.** Geiger runs a double-entry trust ledger in which every line carries dimensions (property, lease, owner and so on). It also has a CAM true-up that posts to the ledger, rent escalation schedules and a no-code workflow builder that runs on the server. The "complete re-architecting" verdict fails too. Most gaps build on existing primitives. Only multi-currency, multi-entity/intercompany, a fund layer, custom objects and a public API touch the core, and the public API first needs real row-level security. Even those extend the current tables rather than replace them. Neither "Geiger Studio" nor Geiger Property has any public web presence. So anything the text says about Geiger was either supplied in its prompt or invented. Its MRI errors read like fluent AI synthesis rather than product knowledge. For the Geiger Property team, the useful output is a short list of gaps worth closing for the buyer Geiger targets, plus a set of concrete defects and stale surfaces the codebase audit turned up along the way.

## Every pillar names a real Geiger gap but misstates MRI

Each claim got two independent checks. The MRI side was checked against MRI's own material: its functional specification, X.5 release notes, product pages and press releases. Most of that is vendor marketing. The functional specification is undated, and G2 blocked automated access. The Geiger side is a read-only audit of this repository at commit `4fdcbd7` (2 November 2026). Each feature was labelled at one of four levels: schema only, data layer, UI, or wired end to end. A fifth label, "capture-only", marks values that are saved but never read by any code. The app was not run in a browser. The live database was not checked against the migration files, because the `geiger-orm` CLI is not installed in this checkout.

The pattern is consistent. On Geiger's side, the list of absences holds up. **Eight of the twelve capability gaps the claim names are fully confirmed, and the other four are partly confirmed.** The claim goes wrong in describing what Geiger *is* (the summary-table rows) and in what it says closing the gaps would take. On MRI's side, **only two claims stand without qualification: multi-currency and CAM reconciliation.** Every other MRI claim either belongs to a separate module, partner product or occupier-side product, or contains a specific detail that MRI's own documentation contradicts or never mentions.

## MRI's capabilities are real but spread across a dozen products

PMX is not a new, APAC-only system. It is MRI's long-standing flagship suite, descended from **"Version X", which MRI launched in October 2014** ([MRI](https://www.mrisoftware.com/news/mri-software-debuts-its-latest-innovative-solution-mri-version-x)). What launched in **May 2024** was a "next-generation PMX" for APAC commercial real estate. That release bundled a UI refresh, the X.7 back end, Studio Suite, a mobile app and Ask Agora. MRI then cited **over 2,500 PMX clients, 50,000 users and more than one million residential units** ([Elite Agent](https://eliteagent.com/mri-software-announces-next-generation-property-management-tech/)). MRI sells PMX worldwide across residential, office, industrial, retail and mixed-use ([MRI US](https://www.mrisoftware.com/products/property-management-x/)). The "ERP" label comes from a customer testimonial ([MRI IE](https://www.mrisoftware.com/ie/products/property-management-x/)); MRI's own copy says "real estate & financials tech platform" ([MRI AU](https://www.mrisoftware.com/au/?p=72174)). "Enterprise ERP" is therefore fair only in the sense of a real-estate ERP covering operations plus full financials. It is not a horizontal ERP in the SAP or Oracle mould.

The deeper problem is packaging. **PMX is licensed by module.** MRI's Ireland page lists Commercial Management, Residential Management, Advanced Retail, Lease Intelligence, a Financials suite and Investment headings under PMX ([MRI IE](https://www.mrisoftware.com/ie/products/property-management-x/)). The US PMX page, by contrast, lists no investment modelling, valuation, debt or waterfall features. It links "Investment Management" as a separate product next to Lease Abstraction ([MRI US](https://www.mrisoftware.com/products/property-management-x/)). Saying "PMX does X" is accurate at the suite level only if the buyer also licenses the right module. For several of the claim's items it is not accurate at all.

### Accounting and commercial: real modules, overstated specifics

On intercompany, MRI's functional specification says Enterprise Ledger "provides support for Entities above the property level" and can "create automated inter-entity offsets and eliminating entries" ([MRI spec](https://www.mrisoftware.com/mrifunctionalspecs/)). That is real automation of the balancing due-to/due-from legs. The phrase "without manual journal entries" appears nowhere in MRI's material, though. The X.5.9 release notes announce that **"manual interentity entries can now be made from the manual journal entry page"** ([MRI release notes](https://info.mrisoftware.com/x5-release-us)). A customer-hosted MRI help instance has a topic titled "Creating Elimination Journal Entries" (seen as a search snippet only, because its certificate had expired) ([MRI WebHelp](https://mrix.kettler.com/MRIWeb/WebHelp/mergedProjects/advgl/EliminationEntries/CreatingEliminationJournalEntries.htm)). A search for the exact phrase returned NetSuite, Oracle and Dynamics marketing, not MRI. This suggests the wording was borrowed from generic ERP copy.

Multi-currency is the one accounting claim that holds cleanly. The GL handles "multiple exchange sources, currency triangulation, and currency translation", and AP has "global multi-currency processing" ([MRI spec](https://www.mrisoftware.com/mrifunctionalspecs/)). Commercial Management keeps entry and base currency in real time (X.5.3), and Budgeting gained multi-currency in X.5.2 ([MRI release notes](https://info.mrisoftware.com/x5-release-us)). One G2 reviewer reports that budgets load only in their native currency, which breaks budget-versus-actual comparisons once figures are consolidated into a single currency ([G2](https://g2.com/products/mri-property-management/reviews/mri-property-management-review-51707)). That evidence is a search snippet with no date.

Tax handling is real but regional. AP has a "robust tax engine", while Malaysia GST and EMEA service charges require purchased Regional Packs ([MRI spec](https://www.mrisoftware.com/mrifunctionalspecs/)). On AP routing, native PMX has "integrated expense approvals for purchase orders, vouchers, payments" and PO "approval limits and override capabilities" ([MRI spec](https://www.mrisoftware.com/mrifunctionalspecs/)). **No public MRI source documents routing by dollar band combined with departmental signing authority.** The fuller AP-automation product was co-developed with Nexus Systems in 2016. **Bottomline, not MRI, acquired Nexus in September 2022** ([FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/paytech/bottomline-acquires-ap-automation-and-real-estate-payments-specialist-nexus-systems)), and in the US MRI partners with AvidXchange ([MRI CA](https://www.mrisoftware.com/ca/?p=23216)).

Commercial recoveries are MRI's strongest ground. A 2019 MRI post describes expense pools, tenant recovery profiles and pro-rata billing of insurance, taxes and maintenance. It also covers monthly estimates drawn from the budget and a year-end reconciliation that posts a one-time charge or credit ([MRI blog](https://www.mrisoftware.com/ie/?p=25458)). Release X.5.3 added recovery caps ([MRI release notes](https://info.mrisoftware.com/x5-release-us)). Gross-up, base-year and admin-fee mechanics are not publicly documented; they are probably present but remain unverified.

Percentage rent and breakpoints are confirmed in Advanced Retail ([MRI](https://www.mrisoftware.com/products/advanced-retail/)). But every source describes sales arriving through tenant self-reporting in Tenant Connect, or through client-supplied data. MRI's Australian retail page promises to "capture turnover automatically" without naming any mechanism ([MRI AU](https://www.mrisoftware.com/au/?p=77215)). MRI OnLocation is the footfall-analytics business that came with Springboard ([MRI](https://www.mrisoftware.com/nz/news/mri-software-acquires-springboard-retail-footfall-ai-analytics/)), not a POS connector. **The "automatically pulls POS data" claim has no support.**

CPI and fixed escalations appear in MRI marketing ([MRI AU blog](https://www.mrisoftware.com/au/blog/how-commercial-property-management-accounting-software-improves-operations/)). Compound fixed-percentage escalation is never documented. The only dedicated "CPI Manager" is in AMTdirect, a lease-accounting product for occupiers ([MRI spec](https://www.mrisoftware.com/mrifunctionalspecs/)).

### Investment, platform and AI: outside PMX, and described inaccurately

The investment pillar compares PMX against a different product line. MRI's investment page names **Asset Modeling** ("run scenarios to hit target IRR"), **Fund Modeling** ("scenario and sensitivity testing"), **Valuation** and **Debt Management**. It states no forecast horizon and never uses the word "predictive". Its only AI mentions sit under business intelligence ([MRI](https://www.mrisoftware.com/products/investment-modeling/)). Valuation applies "UK-specific methodologies and global standards like DCF" ([MRI IE](https://www.mrisoftware.com/ie/?p=57067)). This is deterministic cash-flow modelling driven by assumptions, the same category as ARGUS Enterprise ([Altus Group](https://www.altusgroup.com/solutions/argus-enterprise/)). Neither "predictive analytics" nor "10-to-20-year" has any MRI source. Debt and equity are real and native. Investment Accounting launched in 2016 with capital calls, contributions and "a tailorable waterfall engine", "built within the MRI Financials suite" ([MRI](https://www.mrisoftware.com/news/mri-software-announces-launch-investment-accounting/)). It is still a separate module, not a PMX feature.

The platform pillar fares worst. **No product is called "MRI X."** MRI Agora launched in October 2022 as an "open and connected" platform with a shared data layer ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mri-software-unveils-its-next-generation-real-estate-platform-301658268.html)). The analyst firm Verdantix described it as an Azure data lake plus shared services and a consistent UX. It added that full delivery "is not going to happen overnight" ([Verdantix](https://www.verdantix.com/insights/blog/where-ambition-meets-innovation-mri-software-unveils-new-agora-platform)). Since then Agora has become an AI platform: Ask Agora arrived in October 2025, and Agora Intelligence and Agora Orchestrator in June 2026. Orchestrator runs workflows from a **pre-built agent catalog** ([MRI](https://www.mrisoftware.com/news/mri-software-launches-agora-intelligence-and-agora-orchestrator-delivering-ai-powered-recommendations-and-workflow-execution-for-real-estate/)). That is the opposite of an architecture "built to be heavily modified".

No MRI low-code builder for tables or screens was found. API access goes through the MIX toolkit and Partner Connect, under NDA and possibly for a fee. "There is no self-service developer portal" and no general sandbox ([Apideck](https://developers.apideck.com/apis/accounting/mrisoftware/configuration)). Supergood grades MRI's API an F, though Supergood sells an unofficial MRI API and so has an interest in that grade ([Supergood](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/mri-software)). Salesforce integration runs through custom builds or iPaaS ([Vantage Point](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/sf/unlocking-power-thru-integration-streamlining-commercial-real-estate-operations-with-mri-software-and-salesforce)). **Yardi is MRI's direct competitor.** The only official MRI-Yardi link found covers VaultWare apartment reservations ([Multi-Housing News](https://www.multihousingnews.com/mri-software-to-enhance-its-vaultware-reservation-systems-integration-with-yardi/)). BI runs through Agora's data lake, Informatica's read-only connector ([Informatica](https://onlinehelp.informatica.com/IICS/prod/CDI/en/cloud-data-integration-mri-software-connector/MRI_Software_Connector_overview.html)), or direct SQL access ([Fabric Community](https://community.fabric.microsoft.com/t5/Power-Query/Looking-for-help-connecting-to-MRI-SaaS-data-source/m-p/318446)).

On AI, Contract Intelligence extracts "hundreds of standardized fields" with "multiple layers of expert review" ([MRI](https://www.mrisoftware.com/products/contract-intelligence/)). MRI's own October 2026 description routes uncertain values "to reviewers instead of being accepted automatically" and loads only "validated data" ([MRI blog](https://www.mrisoftware.com/blog/what-is-the-contract-abstraction-process/)). **Hands-free tenant-record creation is not how MRI itself describes its product.** MRI's architecture is AI running over "a shared real estate data foundation" and a "semantic layer" ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/06/mri-software-data-platform-ai-expansion/)). Nothing describes AI inside the database layer.

Searches for "Geiger Studio", "Geiger Property" and the domain geiger.studio found no matching product, listing or review. The only link is this repo's own canonical URL ([app/page.js:7](../app/page.js)). Combine that with the invented "MRI X", the "native" Yardi pipeline and the borrowed NetSuite phrasing, and the text reads as plausible-sounding synthesis. Whoever produced it either had Geiger described to them or filled in the blanks.

## Geiger's ledger already does what the claim's baseline denies

The claim pictures Geiger as "standard cash/accrual books per property" with "fixed monthly residential templates". The code shows something more substantial. **Geiger has a real double-entry general ledger.** It comprises a chart of accounts, journal headers and lines, and a constraint trigger that checks debits equal credits at commit ([ledger_core.sql:87-126, 264-291](../supabase/migrations/20261015212744_ledger_core.sql)). A posting RPC, `ledger_post`, rejects unbalanced entries, entries with fewer than two lines and negative amounts, and de-duplicates on an idempotency key ([ledger_core.sql:299-393](../supabase/migrations/20261015212744_ledger_core.sql)). Voids post mirror-image reversals rather than deleting. A **29-account default chart** includes trust cash accounts, owner equity, contributions, distributions and 4040 CAM Recoveries ([ledger_core.sql:453-528](../supabase/migrations/20261015212744_ledger_core.sql)). The ledger is wired end to end: rent, payments, bills, owner distributions, management fees and deposits all post to it ([lib/ledger/postings.js](../lib/ledger/postings.js)). Server jobs post recurring charges, late fees, management fees and deposit interest idempotently ([lib/jobs/financial.js](../lib/jobs/financial.js)). The ledger feeds trial balance, financial statements, A/P aging and 1099 reports ([defs_financial.js](../lib/reports/defs_financial.js)).

Two details of the claim's baseline are wrong on their face. **Books are per project (workspace), not per property.** Property, unit, lease, tenant, owner, vendor, bank account and job are eight dimensions on each journal line. That is the trust-accounting model built for a management company: one company book with property and owner sub-ledgers. Reporting is accrual-only, with no cash-basis toggle and no period close or lock date. Both are genuine control gaps the claim never mentioned. Geiger's own 16 October 2026 baseline audit says "There is no double-entry ledger." The ledger landed in commit `6c8b76c` that same day, so that document is now stale ([geiger_current_state.md](../research_notes/Property%20management%20software%20features/geiger_current_state.md)).

The pillar-one gaps are nonetheless real. There is **no legal-entity, company or LLC table.** Owners are parties carrying an entity-type label (LLC, Trust and so on). The `property_owners` table links them to properties by ownership percentage ([engine_foundations.sql:91-108](../supabase/migrations/20261015210600_engine_foundations.sql)). `ledger_post` balances per entry, not per entity, and never generates due-to/due-from lines ([ledger_post_line_dimensions.sql:38-53](../supabase/migrations/20261015212915_ledger_post_line_dimensions.sql)). Currency is USD throughout. The only currency column in the schema belongs to Stripe online payments ([online_payments_stripe.sql:34](../supabase/migrations/20261015215336_online_payments_stripe.sql)). At least nine formatters hard-code USD, and the Company Profile picker offers CAD, GBP, EUR, AUD and INR that no code ever reads ([settings/shared.js:53-60](../components/internal/screens/settings/shared.js)). Tax support is a US 1099 summary and W-9 tracking. Bill approval is **single-tier**: one project-wide `billApprovalLimit` marks larger bills "Pending" ([bills.jsx:84-87, 636](../components/internal/screens/accounting/bills.jsx)). Any user with `accounting.post` can then approve, with the approver's name entered as free text ([bills.jsx:651-664](../components/internal/screens/accounting/bills.jsx)). There are no tiers, no approval chain and no signing-authority matrix.

Pillar two is where the claim is most unfair. Geiger has a `commercial` lease type, Commercial and Mixed-use property types, and a real unit square-footage column. **Its CAM reconciliation screen is wired.** Staff tick the expense accounts that form the pool, and actual CAM is summed from the ledger. Each lease's share defaults to its unit's square footage. True-ups post as charges or credits to each lease's ledger under an idempotency key, and can be voided ([cam_reconciliation.jsx:174-270](../components/internal/screens/accounting/cam_reconciliation.jsx)). What it lacks is depth: one pool per property-year, and no caps, base year, expense stop, gross-up, admin fee or per-tenant exclusions. Leases carry a dated rent schedule of fixed-amount or percent steps, which the UI compounds in date order ([lease_money.jsx:95-108](../components/internal/screens/leasing/sections/lease_money.jsx)). **Percentage rent and CPI are entirely absent**: searches for sales reporting, breakpoints, `cpi` and indexation return nothing. The verdict on "fixed monthly residential templates" is therefore *overstated but not baseless*. The commercial lease terms (expense structure, square feet leased, monthly CAM, TI allowance) are capture-only metadata ([leasing/config.jsx:133-139](../components/internal/screens/leasing/config.jsx)). Billed rent remains a single monthly amount.

## Investment, integration and AI gaps hold, with one exception

The investment pillar's Geiger half is accurate. Geiger tracks more than "who owes rent today". It has monthly budgets per GL account with budget-versus-actual variance ([budgets.jsx](../components/internal/screens/accounting/budgets.jsx)) and job costing against capital-project budgets ([job_costing.jsx](../components/internal/screens/accounting/job_costing.jsx)). But nothing looks forward at the asset. Purchase price, current value and assessed value are static inputs that no code reads ([property_tabs.jsx:52-72](../components/internal/screens/properties/sections/property_tabs.jsx)). The asset register stores replacement cost and expected life, but `expectedLifeYears` is never used ([record_kinds.js:111-131](../components/internal/screens/properties/record_kinds.js)). **None of the roughly 85 tables is a loan, fund, investor or valuation table.** The default chart has no Notes Payable, Interest Expense or Depreciation accounts, and the ledger's source-type list has no depreciation or loan type ([ledger_core.sql:73-76](../supabase/migrations/20261015212744_ledger_core.sql)). Equity is the one sub-claim the text overstates. Owner contributions and distributions are real ledger postings, split by ownership percentage in the owner portal ([lib/portal/owner.js](../lib/portal/owner.js)). That is property-owner trust accounting, though, not fund equity with commitments, capital calls or a waterfall. The team's planning document explicitly defers "End-to-end real-estate ERP (investment mgmt + fund accounting)" and "Multi-currency / global entity consolidation" as **P4, out of scope** ([COMPETITIVE_FEATURE_ANALYSIS_2026.md:376-383](../docs/COMPETITIVE_FEATURE_ANALYSIS_2026.md)).

The platform pillar is right about openness and wrong about workflows. **For outside developers, Geiger is closed.** All 43 `app/api` routes are first-party, and there are no API keys, OAuth, SDK, MCP server or CSV import. Webhook endpoints can be saved, but the settings card itself says "nothing is sent" ([integrations.jsx:143](../components/internal/screens/settings/integrations.jsx)). The only live integrations are Stripe ([app/api/stripe/webhook/route.js](../app/api/stripe/webhook/route.js)) and suite email through geiger-dash and Resend ([lib/email/client.js](../lib/email/client.js)). SMS, inbound email, e-signature, screening and listing syndication are stubs or tracking-only ([lib/messaging/deliver.js](../lib/messaging/deliver.js), [lib/supabase/esign.js](../lib/supabase/esign.js)). Custom objects and screens are impossible because `SCREEN_REGISTRY` is a static map from title to component ([registry.jsx:85-180](../components/internal/screens/registry.jsx)).

The exception is the claim that Geiger has no low-code workflows. **Geiger ships a no-code workflow builder that executes.** It covers 7 record types, 12 triggers and 5 step types ([lib/automations/catalog.js](../lib/automations/catalog.js)) on a drag-and-drop React Flow canvas ([workflow_canvas.jsx](../components/internal/screens/automations/canvas/workflow_canvas.jsx)). Database triggers on five tables enqueue events ([messaging_scheduler_automations.sql](../supabase/migrations/20261015212609_messaging_scheduler_automations.sql)), and an idempotent server runner drains them ([lib/automations/runner.js](../lib/automations/runner.js)). Add custom fields (11 types across 10 entities), a company → property → lease policy cascade, lease templates and a permission matrix, and "out-of-the-box, fixed configuration" misleads. A fairer label is **"configurable SaaS with a fixed schema and no external extensibility."**

The AI pillar is simply true. There is no AI, LLM, OCR or PDF-parsing dependency anywhere in [package.json](../package.json). The "AI Assistant" nav item deliberately falls through to a Coming Soon screen ([registry.jsx:86](../components/internal/screens/registry.jsx)). Lease PDFs can be uploaded and stored, but nothing reads them. The only document automation runs the other way, generating leases from templates. AI document parsing sits unchecked in the plan's P3 "AI moat" tier ([COMPETITIVE_FEATURE_ANALYSIS_2026.md:364-372](../docs/COMPETITIVE_FEATURE_ANALYSIS_2026.md)).

## Most gaps bolt on; five touch the core and none needs a rebuild

"Complete re-architecting of its core data structure" is **false**. Geiger's ledger already has the properties that make extension cheap. Lines carry eight dimensions, an entry can have any number of lines, posting is idempotent and enforced by the database, and every table has a `metadata` expansion bag. Child rows can use the generic `record_items` table ([engine_foundations.sql](../supabase/migrations/20261015210600_engine_foundations.sql)), and new automation slots into a job registry. A sales-tax line, a percentage-rent charge or a CAM estimate is just another balanced entry. None needs a change to `ledger_post`. New posting kinds such as depreciation or loan payments need only a constraint migration on the source-type list.

MRI's own packaging supports the point. Its ownership hierarchy above the property and its intercompany eliminations live in **Enterprise Ledger, a separate product layered over a GL whose base is the property entity** ([MRI spec](https://www.mrisoftware.com/mrifunctionalspecs/)). The incumbent extended its ledger; it did not rebuild it.

| Gap | Classification | What closing it involves |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-tier AP approvals | Additive | An `approval_rules` table (amount bands, scope, approver role) and per-bill approval steps; `bills.approval_status` and RBAC already exist |
| CPI indexation | Additive | A CPI index table, a `cpi` step mode with floor/cap/base index, and a job that applies due steps and syncs the billed rent |
| Percentage rent | Additive | A `sales_reports` table, breakpoint terms per lease, and posting through the existing charge builder |
| CAM depth | Additive | Expense pools and per-lease recovery terms (caps, base year, gross-up, exclusions, admin fee); the true-up posting already exists |
| Sales tax / VAT / GST | Mostly additive | Tax codes and rates by jurisdiction, a tax-payable account, and builders that emit tax lines |
| Valuation, depreciation, CapEx plan, loans | Additive | Valuation records, depreciation accounts plus a monthly job, a capital plan reusing `budget_lines`, and a loans table with an amortisation schedule |
| Outbound webhooks, BI, connectors | Additive, after the RLS fix | A delivery job fanning `automation_events` out to stored endpoints; a sync job per connector |
| AI lease abstraction | Additive | Text extraction, an LLM provider (planned as a shared suite package) and a "review extracted fields → create tenant and lease" wizard; lease PDFs first need a private bucket |
| **Multi-entity / intercompany** | Deep extension of the ledger contract | A `legal_entities` table; `entity_id` on accounts, entries, lines, properties and bank accounts; per-entity balancing with automatic due-to/due-from; entity dimension in views; consolidation reports; real per-entity RLS |
| **Multi-currency** | Deepest, cross-cutting | Transaction and functional amounts on every line, exchange rates, FX gain/loss accounts and revaluation; rework of the balance trigger, every posting builder, report, formatter and the Stripe flows |
| **Investor / fund waterfalls** | Structural | A fund and investor layer between owners and properties, a fund dimension on lines, capital calls and a waterfall engine |
| **Public API** | Structural prerequisite | Replace demo-open RLS with project-scoped policies and add API-key/OAuth auth before versioned routes |
| **Custom objects and screens** | Structural | Make the static registry, nav and `view.*` permission keys metadata-driven |

The ranking is an architectural judgment drawn from reading the code. It has not been checked against a backlog or the developers' estimates. On that reading, the additive items are weeks-scale work on existing primitives. Multi-entity and multi-currency are the only items that change the ledger's contract, and even they keep the same tables and RPC pattern. Existing rows could default to one entity per project. **"A significant ledger-engine extension" is the accurate phrase; "complete re-architecting" is not.**

## What the audit means for Geiger Property

### Most of the five pillars are modules Geiger's buyer does not buy

Geiger targets "independent landlords, growing property managers and small-to-mid portfolios" ([landing_page.jsx:19](../components/landing/landing_page.jsx)) and covers "the full residential management lifecycle" ([README.md:17](../README.md)). Against that buyer, most of the claim's pillars describe institutional add-ons. Even the largest vendors split those off. Yardi sells Investment Manager as a **paid add-on** to Breeze Premier ([Yardi Breeze](https://www.yardibreeze.com/pricing/)). ARGUS Enterprise remains the standard tool for DCF and valuation ([Altus Group](https://www.altusgroup.com/solutions/argus-enterprise/)). Investor operations are moving to specialists such as Juniper Square, which claims 2,300+ GPs and does no property management ([Juniper Square](https://www.junipersquare.com/)). MRI fits the same pattern, with separately licensed Investment Management, Enterprise Ledger and Advanced Retail. For a US residential-first product, it is normal to lack fund modelling, waterfalls, valuation engines, multi-currency, VAT/GST engines, retail percentage rent and global intercompany consolidation. Geiger's own plan already defers most of them as P4.

### Four gaps sit on Geiger's own roadmap

Four gaps are worth treating as real because Geiger's own plans or marketing already promise them. The first is **multi-entity**. The planned Scale tier lists "multi-entity" alongside "webhooks/API" ([COMPETITIVE_FEATURE_ANALYSIS_2026.md:411](../docs/COMPETITIVE_FEATURE_ANALYSIS_2026.md)), while the P4 list rules out "global entity consolidation". The team should define the narrower thing a 500-unit management company actually needs before the tier is sold. That is probably separate trial balances per owner LLC inside one project, without intercompany eliminations, and it is the deep ledger extension described above. The second is the **open API and webhooks**, listed as a free P2 differentiator ([COMPETITIVE_FEATURE_ANALYSIS_2026.md:360](../docs/COMPETITIVE_FEATURE_ANALYSIS_2026.md)) but blocked by demo-open RLS. The third is **AI document parsing**, which the plan calls "the 2026 differentiator" and core rather than premium, yet does not exist. MRI's human-review flow is a sensible model to copy. The fourth is **commercial depth**. The landing page markets "Commercial and mixed-use" with CAM billing ([landing_page.jsx:95](../components/landing/landing_page.jsx)). Making the captured commercial terms drive billing, and adding CPI steps, would make that promise honest. Percentage rent can wait for real retail demand.

## Conclusion

Read properly, the pasted claim is a map of buyer segments rather than a list of defects. Each of its five pillars corresponds to a module that MRI itself sells separately, often through acquisitions or partners: Enterprise Ledger, Advanced Retail, the Cougar-derived Investment Management suite, Contract Intelligence, and Nexus/AvidXchange. "Parity with PMX" is therefore not a coherent target for Geiger. The real question is which of those modules a small-to-mid residential manager will pay for. Geiger's own roadmap already gives a partial answer: multi-entity for the Scale tier, an open API, AI document parsing and honest commercial basics. The investment and global-accounting pillars belong where the plan already puts them, out of scope.

The audit's sharpest finding is that none of the pillars is Geiger's critical path. **Row-level security is.** Demo-open RLS and the public document bucket are prerequisites for three of the five pillars: a public API and BI access, entity isolation for multi-entity books, and ingesting confidential lease PDFs for AI abstraction. Ahead of any new capability, the cheapest credibility gains are making existing surfaces tell the truth. Escalations should reach billing, capture-only settings should either do something or disappear, and the stale Integrations screen and README claims should be corrected. A reviewer who reads the product as it stands, as this third-party text evidently did not, should find a product that is smaller than MRI and accurate about itself.
