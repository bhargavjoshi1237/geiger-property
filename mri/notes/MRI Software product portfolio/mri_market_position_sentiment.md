Research date: 2026-11-03. This file builds on two earlier notes and does not repeat them: `research_notes/Property management software features/enterprise_platforms.md` (competitor feature catalogs and pricing) and `research_notes/Geiger vs MRI PMX fact check/mri_platform_ai.md` (Agora, API gating, MIX, Contract Intelligence).

- **Source labels.** Competitor-written pages (Facilio, Infraspeak, Re-Leased, Riooapp) and vendor-interested third parties (Propexo, Supergood) are labeled where cited. MRI's own claims are labeled as vendor claims.

## 1. Customer reviews: ratings, review counts, and recurring praise and complaints by product

### Takeaway
MRI's aggregate ratings look respectable: G2 about 4.1-4.2, Capterra about 4.2 for the flagship, Trustpilot 4.7. Product-level scores are weaker and more scattered:
- **Commercial Management:** 3.7 on Capterra.
- **Property Tree:** 3.7 on Capterra.
- **Affordable Housing:** 3.9 on Capterra (6 reviews) and 3.0 on G2 (1 review).
- **ProLease:** the best of the group, at 4.0-4.4.

The complaints are consistent across products and years:
- Dated, multi-step UI and a steep learning curve.
- Reporting that is hard to find or build.
- Paid consultancy for configuration and custom reports.
- Slow escalations.
- Price rises at renewal.
- Weak mobile apps.

The praise is consistent too: depth, configurability, commercial lease handling, and individual support staff. The best-rated MRI channel, Trustpilot, appears dominated by post-support-interaction reviews of niche products.

### Inferences
- MRI's highest scores sit where reviews are solicited after support tickets (Trustpilot, dominated by MRI_HAB and AU/NZ products). The more representative B2B sites (G2, Capterra) put the core property management products at 3.7-4.2. Commercial Management, Property Tree and Affordable Housing sit at the bottom.
- Value for money is the lowest Capterra sub-score on every MRI product with sub-scores: Residential 3.6, Property Management 3.9, Evolution 3.8. Together with the consultancy and renewal complaints, this is the most consistent sentiment weakness.
- The G2 ease-of-use gap is large and consistent: MRI 7.7-7.8 against AppFolio 9.1. MRI is on par with Yardi Voyager, so MRI is "enterprise-normal" on usability, not uniquely bad. A modern product competes on usability against both of them.
- Support sentiment is polarized. Named account managers and frontline staff are praised, while escalations and the move of work into paid consultancy are criticized. The 2025-26 support-model complaints line up in time with the May 2026 reorganization (see Q4).

### Gaps
- No verified, current G2 or Capterra review counts, and no reviewer-segment splits by company size. All review sites returned 403.
- No product-level ratings found for MRI Palace, ManhattanONE, Qube, Horizon, PMX (as a distinct listing) or MRI Housing (UK).
- No Reddit (r/PropertyManagement, r/CommercialRealEstate) or BiggerPockets material: Reddit is blocked to these tools, and no BiggerPockets threads surfaced.
- Gartner Peer Insights and TrustRadius scores for the main MRI products could not be retrieved.
- Most review quotes are undated in the snippets, so I cannot confirm whether the "outdated UI" complaints predate the PMX UI refresh.

## 2. Pricing evidence, implementation cost and timeline, consultant reliance

### Takeaway
MRI publishes no prices for its core US property management suites. The best hard evidence is MRI's own UK G-Cloud 15 price list (January 2026), which shows:
- Entry packages of about £8k-£10k per year, scaling by lease or property count.
- Per-user licences of £300-£900 per year.
- Separately priced modules, API packs and BI (Agora Insights at 15% of ARR).
- Professional services at £162-£200 per hour.

Implementation takes 4-18 months according to MRI partners, and configuration changes routinely involve consultants. By contrast, AppFolio (from $1.49/unit, 50-unit minimum) and Yardi Breeze ($1/unit, $100 minimum) publish self-serve prices.

### Inferences
- **Small UK agency entry price:** about £8.9k per year (SLM, six users), roughly $11k at current rates. That is equivalent to AppFolio Core on about 600+ units. The vendor-published G-Cloud lists suggest MRI's floor is several times AppFolio's or Breeze's for a sub-500-unit operator, before services at £160-£200 per hour.
- **Commercial lease management:** the Evolution add-on works out to about £267 per lease per year at 30 leases, £250 at 100 and £133 at 300, plus £275 per extra lease. A small commercial landlord with 30-100 leases faces £8k-£25k per year in licence fees alone, on top of a core system and users. Breeze Commercial is $2 per unit per month by comparison.
- **Monetisation pattern:** users, modules, API packs, BI (15% of ARR) and consultancy are all separately monetised. "All-in, per-unit, nothing extra" pricing would be a sharp contrast.
- **Time to value:** implementations measured in quarters, plus consultant dependence for routine configuration, are a structural weakness for buyers without IT staff.

### Gaps
- No US list prices for MRI Residential Management, Commercial Management, PMX or Affordable Housing. No per-unit figures, minimum contract sizes or contract terms (length, auto-renewal, uplift caps) were found.
- No public data on renewal uplift percentages beyond anecdotal review complaints.
- The PMX and Qube PM G-Cloud 14 per-instance prices exist but were not retrieved: the listing returned 404, and Facilio did not quote figures.
- No independent measurement of average MRI implementation duration. All timelines come from consultants or competitors.

## 3. Market share and segments: MRI against Yardi, RealPage, Entrata, AppFolio, Buildium/Rent Manager, ResMan, CoStar/Visual Lease, Accruent, Planon

### Takeaway
MRI is a top-two global property management vendor by claimed units: 15.6M per the vendor in 2025, and 16.5M in its own October 2026 marketing. Its strength is enterprise, mixed-asset, international and specialty portfolios:
- Commercial and lease administration.
- UK social housing and agency.
- Australia/NZ residential agencies.
- US affordable and public housing.
- Large multifamily operators, mostly through bolt-on products.

More than half its revenue is non-US. It has no meaningful presence in the US SMB landlord and self-serve mid-market. That segment belongs to AppFolio, Buildium (RealPage), Rent Manager and Yardi Breeze, which publish pricing and onboard in days or weeks.

### Cited Findings

**Scale and financials**
- MRI's revenue is "approaching $1 billion," with about $400M EBITDA and roughly 10% annual growth. More than half of revenue comes from outside the US. — [Reuters via Yahoo Finance, 2025-09-26](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/exclusive-private-equity-owners-mri-173131792.html)
- **Vendor claims (MRI blog, October 2026):** 16.5M units, 5.5M leases, 45,000+ clients, 170+ countries, 400+ Partner Connect partners; "88% or more of the NMHC Top 50 Managers use MRI solutions." — [MRI blog, 2026-10-09](https://www.mrisoftware.com/blog/which-property-management-software-has-the-best-innovation/)

## 6. Openness and integrations: is "open and connected" borne out?

### Takeaway
Partly. MRI does have a large partner ecosystem (400+ Partner Connect partners by its own count) and sells products that work with competitors' back-offices (Engage for Living on Yardi Voyager). But access is gated and monetised:
- Vendors pay Partner Connect fees of $5k-$15k per year plus a 2.5-15% revenue share, according to Propexo.
- Clients pay about $10k per year for MIX API access.
- In the UK, API packs and BI are priced add-ons.
- There is no self-service developer portal or general sandbox (earlier note).

"Open" here means a curated, paid ecosystem, not an open API platform.

### Cited Findings
- **Partner Connect vs MIX** (Propexo, a unified-API vendor that integrates with MRI):
  - **Partner Connect:** "$5,000–$15,000 annual fee, plus 2.5%–15% revenue share," paid by the vendor. Approval and mutual clients are required, and certification is mandatory. Benefits are a directory listing and marketing enablement.
  - **MIX:** "Free to vendor (MRI client pays $10,000/year for MIX access)." No approval and no certification, and no listing.
  - Source: [Propexo MRI integration paths](https://docs.propexo.com/pms-guidance/mri/integration-paths)
- **Certification:** certified partners receive an annual "health check" call from Partner Connect Operations. Certification yields an "MRI Certified" badge and a directory listing. — [MRI Solution Certification one-sheet](https://www.mrisoftware.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Solution-Certification-One-Sheeter_Website.pdf); [Propexo certification process](https://docs.propexo.com/pms-guidance/mri/certification-process)
- **Partner program structure:** Solution, Consulting, Channel, Alliance and Marketing categories, with annually reviewed tiers. The 2021 guide states no fees. — [MRI Partner Connect Program Guide 2021](https://www.mrisoftware.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/PartnerConnect_ProgramGuide_External_01.04.2021.pdf)
- **Paid API and BI add-ons (UK G-Cloud 15, January 2026):**
  - Separately listed "API Finance Pack" and "API Work Order Pack" for Evolution.
  - Agora Insights at 15% of ARR (£8k minimum) for ManhattanONE on G-Cloud 14.
  - Sources: [G-Cloud 15 Evolution PDF](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-15/documents/709203/231765895062207-pricing-document-2026-01-29-0843.pdf); [Facilio citing G-Cloud 14 (competitor)](https://facilio.com/blog/mri-software-pricing/)
- **Ecosystem scale (vendor claims):** 400+ Partner Connect partners (October 2026); partner network +12% globally and +20% in North America in 2025. — [MRI blog](https://www.mrisoftware.com/blog/which-property-management-software-has-the-best-innovation/); [News on the Block](https://webhook.newsontheblock.com/news-opinion/mri-software-reports-record-year-after-adding-nearly-2-000-new-clients-and-a-surge-in-ai-platform-adoption)

### Inferences
- **Partner tax:** the revenue share passes into what clients pay for integrated services (payments, screening, insurance). The $10k-per-year MIX fee makes even a single custom integration a budget line. Combined with no sandbox, this is a real friction point for both proptech vendors and in-house IT teams.
- **Openness as distribution:** MRI's "open and connected" claim holds for distribution, since partners and cross-PMS products like Engage exist. It does not hold in the developer sense of a public, self-serve, free API.
- **Geiger opportunity:** a public REST API, webhooks, a free sandbox and no revenue share would be a credible "actually open" contrast, both for SMB customers' integrators and for proptech partners.

### Gaps
- MRI has not confirmed the Propexo partner-fee figures, and the fees may vary by product line or region.
- No public list of which MRI products expose which APIs (PMX vs Residential vs Commercial vs Property Tree), and no rate-limit documentation.
- The unit and basis of the "£0.06" figure beside the API Finance Pack could not be determined from the extracted PDF text.

## 7. Synthesis: the 5-10 biggest exploitable gaps for a modern, all-in-one cloud property management app

### Takeaway
MRI's weaknesses cluster around six things:
- Cost transparency.
- Time to value.
- Usability.
- Resident mobile and payments.
- Gated APIs and BI.
- A fragmented, acquisition-built portfolio in a PE exit cycle.

Its strengths (commercial lease administration, enterprise multifamily bolt-ons, UK social housing, AU/NZ agencies, US public housing) are deeply entrenched. Geiger should not attack those head-on.

### Cited Findings
These are the evidence anchors for each gap, cross-referenced from the sections above:

1. **Opaque, enterprise-floor pricing with paid extras.**
   - No public US pricing.
   - UK list entry points of £8k-£10k per year plus per-user licences (£300-£900 per year) and services at £162-£200 per hour.
   - Value for money is the lowest sub-score on every MRI product with sub-scores (3.6-3.9).
   - Reviewers complain of renewal hikes and module-gated capabilities.
   - Sources: [G-Cloud 15 PDFs](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-15/documents/709203/790900596373875-pricing-document-2026-01-28-1713.pdf); [Capterra Residential](https://www.capterra.com/p/226831/MRI-Residential-Management/reviews/); [Facilio (competitor)](https://facilio.com/blog/mri-software-review/)
2. **Long, consultant-dependent implementations.** 4-18 months per MRI partners; configuration changes "typically" need consultants. — [REdirect](https://www.redirectconsulting.com/software-solutions/mri); [Assetsoft](https://assetsoft.biz/mri-implementation); [Riooapp (competitor)](https://riooapp.com/blog/migrate-from-mri-software-what-to-expect-map-watch-out-for)

### Inferences
**Top exploitable gaps for Geiger Property, in rough priority order:**
1. **Transparent, published per-unit pricing that includes reporting, API, mobile and a resident app.** This attacks MRI's lowest-rated dimension (value) and its add-on model directly.
2. **Self-serve onboarding with go-live in days or weeks,** with guided data import (deposits and trust reconciliation, charge codes). This answers the 4-18 month, consultant-led reality.
3. **A modern, consistent UI across residential, commercial and mixed-use in one product,** in contrast to MRI's per-asset-type product sprawl and older UIs.
4. **Built-in reporting with natural-language queries, saved dashboards and exports at no extra cost,** against Agora Insights priced as a percentage of ARR and reports that need specialists.
5. **A first-party, well-rated resident and owner app with native payments,** against RentPayment's 1.5 stars.
6. **A genuinely open API:** public docs, free sandbox, webhooks, no partner revenue share and no per-client API fee.
7. **Mixed-use and small-commercial capability** (CAM reconciliation, lease abstraction-lite, commercial tenant portal) for landlords of roughly 10-300 leases, whom MRI and Yardi Voyager price out and AppFolio and Buildium under-serve.
8. **Responsive in-app support included in the subscription,** framed against MRI's escalation and consultancy complaints during its PE-exit cost cutting.
9. **AI that does work, with visible audit trails, included by default,** rather than a separately sold orchestration layer.

**Segments where MRI is weakest (best targets):** US and English-speaking SMB to lower-mid-market residential fee managers and owner-operators (roughly under 2,000 units), small and mid commercial and mixed-use landlords, and buyers without in-house IT or budget for consultants.

**Segments to avoid head-on:**
- Corporate occupier lease administration (ProLease, Contract Intelligence; IDC Leader).
- UK social housing and public sector (G-Cloud entrenched).
- AU/NZ agencies (Property Tree, Palace, PropertyMe-type local competition).
- US public housing authorities (HAB).
- NMHC Top 50 enterprise operators.

**Timing:** MRI's pending ownership change and cost cutting could make renewing customers receptive to switching. But switching inertia is strong ("not better enough to move away from the last 12 years"), so migration tooling matters as much as features.

### Gaps
- The gap analysis relies on review snippets and competitor-authored summaries for several themes (reporting, support-model change, renewal hikes). Direct verification on G2 and Capterra was blocked.
- There is no win/loss or churn data on MRI customers switching to modern platforms. That data would confirm demand in the target segments.
- No evidence was found on how well PMX's newer UI and Application Studio, where deployed, close the usability gap. If PMX adoption is high, gap 3 narrows for MRI's upgraded base.
